ILLEGAL & UNENFORCEABLE • CA CIVIL CODE § 1954 Line 389

Unannounced Landlord Entry Privileges

What This Means in Plain English

In California, landlords are legally forbidden from entering your home whenever they feel like it. Any clause claiming they can enter "at any hour without notice" is completely void. By law, they must give you at least 24 hours advance written notice, specify reasonable daytime hours (typically 8am–5pm M–F), and hold a valid statutory purpose (repairs, showing unit). Emergency danger to life/property is the only exception.

Statutory Authority: Civ. Code § 1954(c) explicitly bars non-emergency unauthorized inspections. Under Civ. Code § 1953(a)(1), any lease agreement term modifying or waiving tenant rights under § 1954 is void as contrary to public policy.

VOID AS AGAINST PUBLIC POLICY • CIVIL CODE § 1942.1 Line 512

Habitability Warranty Cannot Be Shifted to Tenant

What This Means in Plain English

A landlord is strictly required by California law to maintain structural integrity, weatherproofing, working hot water, heating, and functional plumbing. Even if you signed this agreement acknowledging the "as is" clause, the clause has zero legal weight in any court. Landlords cannot transfer statutory structural repair duties to residential tenants.

Controlling Precedent: Green v. Superior Court (1974) 10 Cal.3d 616 guarantees the non-waivable implied warranty of habitability. Civil Code § 1942.1 declares any agreement waiving rights under § 1941 void against California public policy.

EXCESSIVE LIQUIDATED DAMAGES • CIVIL CODE § 1671(d) Line 204

Compounding Daily Late Penalties Are Unlawful

What This Means in Plain English

Late fees cannot be used to punish you or generate landlord profit. Charging a flat $25 per day that compounds daily creates an illegal punitive penalty that easily surpasses the actual cost of a delayed payment. California courts consistently strike down compounding daily late fees in residential leases.
